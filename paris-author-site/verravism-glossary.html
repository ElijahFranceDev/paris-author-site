A public reference for writers, readers, educators, reviewers, and booksellers. The terms describe this movement’s framework; familiar literary language also has meanings outside Verravism.

Method and examples · Laws and scale

Anchor Rule
Preserve orientation in the central action, goal, setting, and stakes despite deliberate distortion.
Baseline
Controlled Chaos level 1: recognizable narrative form.
Belief Ledger
A planning and revision record of belief, evidence, reader inference, and consequence.
Breach
Controlled Chaos level 3: rare, earned disruption of form.
Certainty Fracture Method
Change the interpretation of previously established evidence.
Character Witness
What a character believes, remembers, or claims.
Controlled Chaos
P.M. France’s signature method of calibrating narrative and page pressure.
Distortion
Controlled Chaos level 2: selective pressure on the established form.
Disturb
Introduce an inconsistency with a plausible explanation.
Establish
Build a credible normal state and assumption.
Five Movements of Certainty
Establish, Disturb, Fracture, Reconstruct, Remain; functions that may recur.
Fracture
Force a meaningful challenge to an established belief.
Fractured Certainty
A consequential shift in an established understanding, not mere confusion.
Mnemonic
Uncertainty about memory and the past.
Moral
Uncertainty about guilt, loyalty, justice, or responsibility.
Moral Reassignment
Reframe the ethical interpretation of an unchanged action.
Narrative Echo Method
Repeat something ordinary with evolving significance.
Negative Evidence
Give a meaningful absence or omission narrative weight.
Perceptual
Uncertainty about experienced reality.
Reader Witness
The interpretation formed by a reader using evidence and assumptions.
Reader’s Mark
A visual identifier for voluntary Verravic Fiction self-identification, not certification.
Reconstruct
Form a revised interpretation using the available evidence.
Relational
Uncertainty about relationships, trust, love, or dependency.
Remain
Complete an immediate narrative movement while preserving its consequential residue.
Residual Certainty
Resolve the main narrative while leaving a meaningful belief unsettled.
Seven Craft Standards
Belief, Pressure, Change, Cost, Control, Fairness, and Restraint.
Structural
Uncertainty involving the presentation of the text.
Systemic
Uncertainty shaped by institutions and records.
Text Witness
The information the narrative presents, withholds, or organizes; it is not necessarily an objective record.
Textual Witnessing
Use the form or material of the text as narrative evidence.
Three-Witness Principle
Character, text, and reader occupy distinct positions of understanding.
Verravic Fiction
Fiction applying the philosophy across traditional genres.
Verravic Fracture Scale
Five descriptive intensity levels, distinct from Controlled Chaos levels.
Verravic Method
The overall craft framework for belief, pressure, change, and consequence.
Verravic Narrative Architecture
A unified planning sequence connecting witnesses, methods, movements, and review.
Verravism
The literary movement and philosophy of fractured certainty.
Verravist
A writer or practitioner who voluntarily uses the framework.
Witness Delta
A meaningful gap between witness positions.
Witness Displacement
Shift the reader’s trust between witness positions.
