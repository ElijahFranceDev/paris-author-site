Practice the seven methods

Witness Displacement

Write 300 words in which a trusted speaker and an overlooked speaker describe the same event. Keep the narrator unchanged. Add one observable fact that changes trust.
Self-check: The new trust follows evidence rather than sudden villainization.

Certainty Fracture

Write a scene around a locked door. Establish one reasonable purpose, then reveal another without changing the door or its physical condition.
Self-check: Both readings fit the earlier evidence; the revised reading changes a decision.

Narrative Echo

Use the same sentence or object three times in a 500-word story. Its meaning must change each time.
Self-check: Each repetition has a new context and consequence.

Negative Evidence

Describe a familiar routine. Omit one expected response or object, then provide a reason the absence matters.
Self-check: The expectation is established before the omission; the explanation is earned.

Moral Reassignment

Write an apparent betrayal in 200 words, then a 200-word discovery that reframes the act while preserving the facts.
Self-check: Readers can reconsider without being told what moral judgment to adopt.

Textual Witnessing

Write a scene with a character’s claim and a short dated note that contradicts it. Express the evidence in words as well as layout.
Self-check: A reader using plain text or audio can still understand the contradiction.

Residual Certainty

End a short story with the immediate problem resolved but a relationship or moral interpretation unsettled.
Self-check: The plot is complete; the open question has meaningful stakes.

A seven-day practice plan

  1. Day 1: draft a belief and its consequence.
  2. Day 2: make a Three-Witness ledger.
  3. Day 3: practice Witness Displacement and Negative Evidence.
  4. Day 4: practice Narrative Echo and Textual Witnessing.
  5. Day 5: practice Certainty Fracture and Moral Reassignment.
  6. Day 6: write an ending with Residual Certainty.
  7. Day 7: combine two methods in one complete story and revise using the Five Laws.
