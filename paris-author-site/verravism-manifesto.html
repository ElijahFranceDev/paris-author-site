Verravism: The Literature of Fractured Certainty

Public founding edition 1.0. The twelve declarations below reproduce the founding manifesto from P.M. France's project source bible, dated for this public edition November 25, 2026.

I. The reader is a witness.

The reader does not merely consume a plot. They form beliefs, choose whom to trust, overlook details, and judge what is fair. A Verravic story may hold those choices up for examination.

II. Certainty is conditional.

Memory, identity, loyalty, evidence, and morality can change meaning when the reader gains information. The change must be earned.

III. Feeling precedes theory.

Grief, fear, shame, desire, love, pride, or obsession must make uncertainty matter. The genre is not a puzzle without people.

IV. The ordinary can carry the disturbance.

An everyday object or sentence may acquire a second meaning. The first appearance should be natural, not announced as a code.

V. The page may testify.

Form can reinforce the character's pressure. Density, repetition, space, chronology, and a chapter's shape may act as evidence or misdirection.

VI. Control is non-negotiable.

The author must know which details are intentionally unstable and which are simply erroneous. Chaos is designed.

VII. Language belongs to people.

A character's voice must be human, particular, imperfect, and credible. Literary method never overrides social reality or personality.

VIII. Contradictions have consequences.

A broken assumption should alter action, relationship, self-understanding, or the reader's moral position.

IX. Readers deserve a way through.

Codes and hidden layers can reward close attention but must not obstruct the basic causal and emotional story.

X. Endings must do work.

An ending may leave a mystery unresolved while completing an immediate emotional, moral, or narrative movement.

XI. The writer must protect the work.

Fix avoidable errors in names, chronology, speaker identity, object location, and action. Preserve only deliberate uncertainty.

XII. Verravism is not owned by one subject.

A Verravic novel may be urban crime, family drama, romance, horror, fantasy, or historical fiction. It need not share a universe or include the supernatural.

The character may lie. Memory may lie. The page may lie. The reader must still be able to find the story underneath all three.
